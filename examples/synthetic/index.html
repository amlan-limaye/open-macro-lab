Interpretation

Read the components before the headline.

The latest complete Wednesday is 2025-04-23. Over four calendar weeks the selected liquidity proxy rose by 60 USD bn. Fed assets had the largest absolute accounting contribution (40 USD bn). Observed reserves changed by 32 USD bn, while the proxy–reserve gap changed by 28 USD bn. These movements describe selected balance-sheet positions; they do not identify a policy shock or establish an asset-price forecast.

The subtraction is accounting, not a causal model. The proxy is neither global liquidity nor a measure of investable money.

02 / Accounting drivers

Where the change came from

Calendar endpoint differences · same snapshot vintage

Four-week contribution to the proxy

An increase in assets adds to the proxy. An increase in the Treasury account or total reverse repos subtracts from it. Contributions sum exactly to the proxy change.

How to read this

Treasury cash accumulation and reverse-repo balances occupy liability-side space. Holding other items fixed, their decline raises this proxy.

Actual reserves can move differently as currency, other deposits, liabilities, and capital change. The second recipe measures that difference; it does not explain each omitted line.

03 / Executable recipe notebook

Two recipes. Explicit limits.

Download method JSON
Recipe 01 / Selected balance-sheet liquidity

Decompose a narrow US liquidity proxy

L = (WALCL − WDTGAL − WLRRAL) / 1000

WALCL is consolidated Fed assets; WDTGAL is the Treasury general account; WLRRAL is total reverse-repo liabilities, including foreign official and international accounts. Each is millions of USD; divide by 1,000 once.

This definition intentionally differs from the common formula using only overnight reverse repos. It preserves one source, one observation basis, and a clear accounting perimeter.

Recipe 02 / Reserve cross-check

Test the proxy against observed reserves

R = WRBWFRBL / 1000
G = L − R

WRBWFRBL is the Wednesday reserve-balance level. G is the difference between the selected proxy and reserves. Its movement quantifies how much the proxy and actual reserves diverged.

The residual includes other liabilities and capital. It is not a statistical error and is not itself a measure of monetary accommodation. The weekly-average series WRESBAL is deliberately excluded.

Alignment, dates, uncertainty, and economic limitations

All inputs must be USD millions, unadjusted, weekly Wednesday levels. We join on the exact observation date and retain absent weeks as missing. We never fill a holiday, copy a later value backward, or treat a missing value as zero.

A date in the CSV is the observation date. Historical publication timestamps are unknown in this release. Retrieval times and HTTP dates are capture evidence only. Charts use the latest values in the selected snapshot, including any revisions already present.

  • US Federal Reserve balance sheet only; not global liquidity, broad money, private credit, or tradable cash.
  • Total reverse repos include foreign official and international accounts and other reverse repos. This is not the popular assets-minus-TGA-minus-ON-RRP formula.
  • The gap includes omitted liabilities and capital; it is not an unexplained statistical error or a complete reconciliation of every H.4.1 line.
  • Current-vintage history is unsuitable for a historical point-in-time backtest. Observation dates do not establish publication or availability dates.
  • Per-observation historical publication dates and vintages are not retrieved. Snapshot comparisons establish changes seen between captures, not the time or reason an official revision occurred.
  • The 10-calendar-day stale threshold is an operational warning, not a release-calendar forecast. Same-day captures can straddle an upstream update.
  • A rising proxy does not establish easier financial conditions or predictive market returns. No live trading or investment recommendation.
